Our Lady of Lourdes

1858 · St. Bernadette Soubirous · Feast, February 11

The grotto where Our Lady appeared to Bernadette eighteen times in 1858 is now a place of prayer and care for the sick that welcomes pilgrims from around the world.

What happened here

The Story

On February 11, 1858, fourteen-year-old Bernadette Soubirous went with her sister Toinette and a friend to gather firewood by the Gave river. At the grotto of Massabielle she saw a young woman dressed in white, with a blue sash, a rosary on her arm and a yellow rose on each foot. Bernadette took out her own rosary and prayed. The Lady prayed with her in silence and then disappeared.

The Lady appeared eighteen times between February and July. On February 18 she spoke for the first time, asking Bernadette to come to the grotto for fifteen days, and told her, "I do not promise to make you happy in this world, but in the other." Crowds began to follow Bernadette to the grotto, along with the police, who questioned her repeatedly.

On February 25 the Lady told Bernadette to drink from a spring. She dug at the muddy ground until water came; that spring still flows and supplies the taps and baths used by pilgrims today. On March 2 the Lady asked that priests come in procession and that a chapel be built. The parish priest, Abbé Peyramale, answered that he needed to know her name.

On March 25, the feast of the Annunciation, the Lady said in the local dialect, "Que soy era Immaculada Councepciou": "I am the Immaculate Conception." Bernadette did not understand the words and repeated them all the way to the priest's house. The dogma had been defined by Pope Pius IX only four years earlier. The last apparition took place on July 16, 1858.

Bernadette later joined the Sisters of Charity of Nevers, where she lived quietly until her death on April 16, 1879. She was canonized on December 8, 1933.

How to read this account

Church Recognition

In July 1858 Bishop Bertrand-Sévère Laurence of Tarbes set up a commission of clergy and scientists to examine Bernadette, the witnesses and the reported cures. After nearly four years, on January 18, 1862, he declared in a pastoral letter that the faithful are "justified in believing" that the Immaculate Mary appeared to Bernadette.

The tradition

Bernadette's own testimony, given repeatedly to clergy and civil officials in 1858, and the accounts of witnesses present at the grotto.

Church judgment

Declared worthy of belief by the local bishop in 1862. The feast was extended to the whole Church in 1907.

Reported healings

Claimed cures are examined by the Lourdes Medical Bureau, founded in 1883, and then by the bishop of the healed person. Around seventy have been declared miraculous.

Catholic teaching

Approved private revelations are not required belief. They can help the faithful live the Gospel; they add nothing to it.

Popes have come as pilgrims, including St. John Paul II in 1983 and 2004 and Benedict XVI in 2008 for the 150th anniversary of the apparitions.

    How long to stay

    Suggested lengths of stay

    1 day

    The Basilica of St. Francis, Santa Chiara, and the Sanctuary of the Renunciation. Possible from Rome, but rushed.

    2–3 days

    Adds San Damiano, the Carceri hermitage and the Portiuncula, with time for Mass and quiet.

    5 days

    Adds day trips to Orvieto, Cascia and Norcia. Pairs naturally with Rome.

    Getting around

    Local travel

    • Assisi's train station is in Santa Maria degli Angeli, below the old town. A local bus climbs to the gates.
    • The old town is steep and partly cobbled. Allow extra time with anyone who walks slowly.
    • Perugia airport (PEG) is about 20 minutes away; most U.S. travelers arrive via Rome.
    • A car makes Cascia and Norcia practical; Orvieto is also reachable by train.
